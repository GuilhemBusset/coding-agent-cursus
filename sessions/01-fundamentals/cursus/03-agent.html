Speaker notes

The 2026 landscape

Each rung hands more of the loop to the agent.

Each rung up, you review fewer steps and bigger results: your checks must climb too.

Speaker notes

Talk track: five ways to work with a model, bottom to top; each column is a step of our loop, numbered as on the spine. Point at rung 1: the model finishes your line; you do the rest. Point at rung 2, chat: the model wrote the code; you ran it, pasted the error back, decided to ask again and when to stop. You were the harness. Point at the arrows up into rung 3, today, where we are: the harness runs, pastes back and stops; the model decides; the harness lets safe steps through and asks you about the rest. Point at the next arrow: on rung 4 the run happens in a cloud sandbox, usually with nobody watching, and returns a pull request. Rung 5 adds scale, not another step: many such loops at once, reviewed as a batch.

Point at the last column: fewer steps to watch, bigger results to check: tests, a contract, CI.

The 2026 landscape

The tasks agents finish keep getting longer.

Source: METR, Time Horizon 1.1, metr.org, 2026; data: metr.org/time-horizons.

Speaker notes

Talk track: METR, an independent lab, times tasks on skilled humans, then runs models on them in its own harness. Point at the bottom left: GPT-4, 2023, about 4 minutes; each gridline is about four times the one below. Follow the dashed line, METR’s own fit: it doubles about every four months. Point at the right: Claude Opus 4.6, early 2026, about 12 hours, interval 5 to 61 hours. The hollow point, Mythos Preview, sits above 16 hours, where the suite is unreliable.

Read-back question: “Does 12 hours mean the agent ran for 12 hours?” No: it is how long those tasks take a skilled human; the agent finishes them half the time.

Caveats, aloud: the “every 7 months” you may have read is since 2019; since 2024 it is nearer 3. 50% is a coin flip: at 80%, Opus 4.6 manages about 70 minutes. The tasks are cleaner than your repo. And these are METR’s numbers as of 8 September 2026, when the page stopped being updated.

Your turn on P00, graded by the evidence, not the message.

Speaker notes

Talk track: read the headline. Point at the band: everything but the model is the harness, and the harness is yours to set. Each badge has opened into a habit, in order:

  • One, who runs the tools: watch what it runs. The commands are the evidence, not the story around them.
  • Two, what each call sees: one session per task, since every old turn rides along on every call. A long read can go to a subagent, in its own window.
  • Three, what loads before you type: keep the prefix lean. A short AGENTS.md that says how to run the tests; only the MCP servers you use.
  • Four, what may it run: grant the least it needs, and a hook for any rule that must hold.
  • Five, when it stops: “done” is a claim: check it, with the test run and the diff.

Predict question, on the callout: “In Act IV you drive. When the agent says it is done, what do you look at first?” Listen for: the test output, the diff.
