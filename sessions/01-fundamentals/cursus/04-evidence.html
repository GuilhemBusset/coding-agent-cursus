Speaker notes

Talk track: plan mode is the least you can grant: the agent reads and proposes, and it cannot edit a file. Point at the grid: one row per start day, one column per day on duty. Point at the list: what a good plan for this problem has.

Predict question: “Monday: which two start days are off?” Press the majority’s chip, then Reveal.

Tuesday and Wednesday: a Tuesday block ends on Saturday, a Wednesday block on Sunday. So Monday’s row in the plan reads x Thursday plus Friday, Saturday, Sunday and Monday, at least 8. A plan without the wrap comes back later as a feasibility: line from the checker; continuous starts as integrality: lines. Here it costs one sentence to the agent.

Activity: start plan mode, send the prompt, and check the plan against the list. Pairs compare their Monday rows. Something missing? Say so and let the agent revise; only then leave plan mode.

Step 4 · Check

Why no roster can use fewer than 11 people.

8+7+8+9+8+5+6 = 51 person-days ÷ 5 days each = 10.2 → whole people: 11

Speaker notes

Talk track: point at the sum. The week needs 51 person-days; one person gives five. So any roster needs at least 51 over 5, 10.2 people, and people are whole: at least 11. Your PASS roster has 11, so nothing beats it. Point at the axis: below 10.2 no roster fits. That is the summary under PASS: LP bound 10.2, known optimum 11, gap 0.8.

Finding a roster took a solver; proving it best took one sum. That gap is why checking pays: generating is hard, checking is cheap. Operations research has long worked this way, with bounds, gaps and certificates. Tonight’s P01 is the same at scale: HiGHS needs seconds, and the checker replays your plan with no solver and prints the gap to the known optimum.

Activity: say the sum with the room: eight, fifteen, twenty-three, thirty-two, forty, forty-five, fifty-one; over five, 10.2; round up, 11.

Transition: “It passes, and you know why. Now ship it, the way you will every week.”

Background for you, not for the slides: the README’s “Bound and optimum”; check.py pins LP_BOUND = 10.2 and KNOWN_OPTIMUM = 11, derived by hand; the dual y = 1/5 on every day certifies the bound.
