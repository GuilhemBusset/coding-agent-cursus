Poll · Predict, lock in, then reveal

Did AI make experienced developers faster?

In METR’s randomized trial with early-2025 AI tools, experienced open-source developers forecast that AI would make them 24% faster. What did METR measure?

19% slower: with AI allowed, they took 19% longer to complete their issues. Even after the slowdown, they still believed AI had sped them up by about 20%.

Source: METR, Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity, metr.org, published 2025-07-10. A randomized controlled trial: 16 developers, 246 issues from their own repositories.

Poll · The 2026 follow-up

The 2026 follow-up: weak evidence, by METR’s own account.

Group (developers)METR’s estimateConfidence interval
Early 2025 (16)tasks “take 19% longer”+2% to +39%
Late 2025, returning (10)“a speedup of -18%”-38% to +9%
Late 2025, new (47)“speedup is -4%”-15% to +9%

“Our raw results show some evidence for speedup”, and METR believes developers are likely “more sped up from AI tools now”.

Selection bias

  • Developers opted out rather than work without AI.
  • 30% to 50% of those surveyed held back tasks they would not do without AI.
  • Pay fell from $150/hr to $50/hr.

METR: “an unreliable signal”, “only very weak evidence”, likely “a lower-bound”.

The lesson is neither number: measure, don’t trust the feeling.

Source: METR, We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design, metr.org, published 2026-02-24. Quoted verbatim, signs as METR gives them.

The jagged frontier

Better inside the frontier, worse just outside, and the edge is invisible.

A preregistered experiment with Boston Consulting Group: 758 knowledge workers, randomly assigned to no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 with a prompt engineering overview.

  • Inside the frontier (18 realistic consulting tasks): AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% more quickly on average, with “significantly improved quality”.
  • Outside it (one complex managerial task): AI users were “19% less likely to produce correct solutions”.

For you: tasks of a seemingly similar difficulty can fall on either side. Nobody sees the edge without checking.

Source: Fabrizio Dell’Acqua et al., Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, Organization Science 37(2), March 2026, doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838. The one discipline

The one discipline of this cursus

Never raise an agent’s autonomy past your ability to verify the solution, not just the code.

In our world the program can be green while the model is silently wrong: a flipped objective sense runs, passes a weak test and returns a number.

It goes on the board now and stays there all day. Every act ends by telling you how it changes the way you drive.

How we work

Three modes, two sticky notes, mixed pairs.

  • ModeWatchThe instructor demos. Note what the agent claims.
  • ModePredictCommit and lock in before the reveal.
  • ModeDriveHands on your own agent.
  • Sticky notes: green on your screen means done or fine; red (or pink) means stuck, and someone comes to you.
  • Pairs: mixed where possible, one with Claude Code and one with Codex. Each drives the agent they have; you swap at L3 in Act IV.

You have Claude Code or Codex, not necessarily both. Every instruction today works in either.
